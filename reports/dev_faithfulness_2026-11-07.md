# Is the reader's p faithful to the annotators? (dev)

Issue #119, plan §7.2, spec §3.7 (E6's conventions). Dev's original file (commit `8a3a52b`) keeps every annotator's answer, so for each question we can measure q, the share of its annotators who gave each answer, and compare it with the reader's probabilities p. Rows 0 and 1, DeBERTa-v3-large, 3 seeds each, from each run's saved top-10 candidates at its validation temperature.

Generated by `python sandbox/bruhmiuz/dev_faithfulness.py reports/dev_faithfulness_2026-11-07.json`. Its docstring defines each measure. The candidates aren't committed (workflow.md §2.2), so it runs where they are.

**Use of dev.** Spec rule 1 limits dev to final scoring. This ran after every system was frozen and scored (#115, merged in #127), and it fits and chooses nothing: no setting, temperature or system changes because of it. Yuhao decided to go ahead on 7 Nov; teammates who object can say so on the PR.

## Findings

1. **The reader's probabilities match the annotators' shares closely.** Over every top-10 answer group, binned by p, the mean share of annotators differs from the mean p by 0.003 on row 0 and 0.008 on row 1, weighted by bin size. When row 1 gives an answer 0.55, 55% of annotators chose it. This is the first direct support for the assumption behind MBR, beyond average calibration.
2. **Only the confident end is off, and row 1 is a little overconfident there.** Answers with p above 0.9 get 0.93 of the annotators on average on both rows (p 0.957 on row 0, 0.962 on row 1), and row 1's 0.7–0.9 bins fall 0.04 short. Part of the close fit for the top answer is built in: the temperature was fitted on validation against one annotator per question, and if that annotator is like the others, they agree with the top answer with probability q(top). The gap over all groups, not only the top one, is the stronger evidence.
3. **The joint head is slightly closer to q overall, but not where annotators disagree.** The Jensen–Shannon distance is 0.259 on row 1 against 0.268 on row 0 (−0.009 [−0.011, −0.008]). On the 4,773 questions with two or more answer groups, the two are level (0.324 and 0.322), and KL(q‖p) is 0.43 on both rows over the questions where all of q's groups are in the top 10 (86–88% of them).
4. **Under q, MBR still doesn't beat MAP, as on the test set.** Scored by expected EM + F1 over the annotators, MBR loses to MAP on row 0 (−0.36 [−0.49, −0.23]) and ties on row 1 (−0.05 [−0.15, +0.05]). On the questions where annotators disagree, row 1's MBR gains F1 (+0.12 [+0.03, +0.20]) and ties on EM + F1 (+0.04 [−0.13, +0.21]). So the reader's p is close to q, yet the decision that is optimal under p = q doesn't win.
5. **Why it doesn't, and what that means.** MBR's utility is our content-word F1 and EM between positions, while q scores with the official string scorer. MBR also sees only the top 10, and q from three annotators is coarse. Plan §7.2's table predicts MBR wins when p = q and the utilities agree; here the first holds roughly and the second doesn't. The ceiling is high: the best of the top 10 under q scores about 82 EM and 91 F1, against 74 and 87 for either decoder.

## Limits

- Three annotators make q coarse: most values are 1/3, 2/3 or 1.
- Annotators disagreeing isn't necessarily the true uncertainty of the answer. q also mixes in boundary conventions and mistakes.
- p is known only on the top 10. KL is exact only on questions where every annotated group is in the top 10, and the Jensen–Shannon distance is a lower bound on the others.
- Only the fitted temperature is reported. Recomputing p at T = 1, as the issue asked, needs every span's score, because the top 10 don't give the normalizer at another temperature. That would take a GPU rescoring job, which this check doesn't justify.
- 91 of 34,726 annotations aren't word-aligned. They are left out of q's groups, but still count in the expected EM and F1, which use the official scorer on the texts.
- Expected scores under q average over annotators, so they are lower than the official dev score, which takes the best over references (row 1 MAP: 73.8 EM under q, 90.5 official).
